TL;DR (v3, honest)

  • $2,000 USD in. Aakash paid for the InspectMind ULI engagement. First external revenue. Validates the $2K price anchor (matches Cardinal's floor exactly).
  • Skylarq's own outbound: 0.28% reply rate on 6,072 sends. InspectMind day-1 looked similar (0.5% CTR on 366 sends) — but partially explained by client copy decisions we knew were wrong and conceded to anyway.
  • The central question shifts: not "can we sell outbound as a service?" but "can we sell it with the discipline to refuse yes-man mode?" Cardinal does this at $2K × 30 clients. We're not there yet.
  • 2 of our own campaigns auto-paused for bounces in early May. Even with internal QA, deliverability isn't solved.
  • The April 21 audit caught 38 already-shipped emails with material errors (24 spintax leaks, 14 cert-as-firstname). Internal QA framework was BUILT in response.
  • Phillip-hours estimate on InspectMind: 30-60 hours over 5 weeks for $2K revenue. ~$33-67/hour effective rate. Service-economics, not SaaS-economics — yet.
  • Stage 9 (client copy iteration) cannot be automated in its current form. Aakash had 5-6 explicit contradictions across feedback rounds. AI can't predict what a founder will object to in round 4 vs round 2 — they themselves don't know.
  • ~70% of the 20 stages ARE automatable. But "automatable" ≠ "valuable" — Apollo + Instantly + Claude already do most of it commodity-style.
  • Personalization isn't always required (Mudit's counter-thesis). Templated emails run the industry. For acute-pain B2B, generic outperforms vague-personal. Reconsider where we spend personalization budget.
  • We eat blame regardless of client decisions. Push back on copy → client insists → conversion misses → we own the miss. Structural agency reality. Defensive moves needed in SOW.
  • Service business vs VC-backable is the unresolved strategic question. Jack's vision: AI agent infrastructure with humans only at high-judgment moments. Phillip's pushback: we're still capped by my bandwidth as the human in Stage 9.
  • Honest product hypothesis: Vertical outbound managed service for founder-led companies in 1-2 verticals. $2-5K/mo retainer with $2K floor (Cardinal model). Per-email-inbox cap on volume. Refuse ACV < $5K clients.

10 Service business vs venture-backable — THE central question

Surfaced in the May 12 standup. The unresolved strategic question.

Jack's vision (AI agent as service)
  • 90% of the 20 stages mechanized via AI agents
  • Humans only get pinged at high-judgment moments
  • Infrastructure on AWS, drop client info into execution loops
  • Scales to hundreds of clients with bounded human labor
  • Path to VC-backable valuation: it's "AI infrastructure" not "agency"
  • Cardinal already does this at small scale (20-30 clients, $2K floor, very happy customers)
Phillip's pushback (we're still a service business)
  • Stage 9 (client copy iteration) is the bottleneck — currently 15-25h per engagement, ALL Phillip
  • Even at 5x productivity vs pre-2025, Phillip is still the human in the loop
  • The leverage AI provides is "do services more efficiently" — but the service still scales with humans
  • Agencies are valued at 0.5-1.5× ARR by investors. VC won't fund this.
  • Can't sell to investors as SaaS. We'd be lying about the unit shape.
The honest split

What both Jack and Phillip agree on: in the next 1-2 years, AI agents WILL mechanize more of the 20 stages. The question is whether Stage 9 — the irreducibly-human copy-iteration loop — can be reduced from 15-25h to 5-10h via better tooling (Slack bot for client interaction, structured-feedback templates, etc.).

If yes: we have a venture-backable infrastructure play. 100 clients × $5K/mo retainer × 80% gross margin = $4.8M annual gross profit. Real business.

If no: we're an agency that uses AI to be 5x more productive than competitors. Still profitable, but $50-200K annual gross profit per Phillip. Lifestyle business, not VC-backable.

Status today: we're closer to the second. The path to the first runs through mechanizing Stage 9. That's the open R&D bet.

Phillip's standup framing

"Despite the fact that I'm 5x more productive, I'm still the human in the loop. And in that case, it's hard to envision that we can truly productize myself."

"If you ask me which segment, I'd be confident in doing conference follow-up vs pure cold outbound — at least we have warm context to leverage."

Pricing model — recommended for Wedge A

  • Base retainer: $2,000/mo floor (Cardinal benchmark). Goes up based on volume + complexity.
  • Per-meeting bonus: $200-500 per qualified meeting booked.
  • Per-email-inbox cap: price tiered by # of dedicated mailboxes (5/10/20/40). Natural volume cap. Prevents Vishal-abuse pattern (client wants to email 10K leads/month).
  • Custom domains per client: ~$100-200/mo added to retainer. Protects Skylarq's pool.
  • Modular scope: "$X for this conference; anything else is additional." Cardinal model. Refuse scope creep.
The Vishal-abuse pattern

Standup observation: clients given unlimited email volume will abuse it. Vishal example: "He seems like the type who'd want to reach out to every VP, then every PM, then everyone on the team. Give him 1K leads, he wants another 1K."

Per-email-inbox pricing creates an artificial volume cap. Client controls how many leads they reach by paying for more mailbox capacity. Forces ICP discipline.

14 What we eat blame for regardless

Structural reality of agency work, surfaced in the May 12 standup. Worth surfacing as its own section because it shapes SOW design.

For the Aakash engagement, Phillip pushed back explicitly on several copy decisions Aakash insisted on:

  • Pitchy E1 with discount code (Aakash's v12) — Phillip advised softer warm-context opener; Aakash wanted pitch

The structural problem

When conversion misses, the client doesn't say "I made the call against your advice." The client says "you didn't deliver." Phillip standup: "It doesn't matter what — we're going to take the blame for it even if HE fucked that up. He's like, 'oh, you guys didn't convert anything for me.' Like, he's going to say that."

Defensive moves to add to SOW

  1. Every disputed decision in writing — Slack DM with "you asked X, I recommended Y, you confirmed X anyway." Linked from the SOW.
  2. Conversion benchmarks set upfront — "expected reply rate at standard quality is 1-3%. Below that, we investigate. Below 0.5%, we re-engage on copy direction."
  3. Client-facing dashboard — Aakash sees the same numbers we see. No surprise at end-of-engagement.
  4. Out-clause for client copy override — "if you override our copy recommendations against our advice, conversion miss is not refundable." Hard but necessary.

16 Scope we are NOT addressing

Explicit scope discipline from the standup. The current Wedge A covers ONE channel:

  • Email only. No LinkedIn. No Twitter. No phone. No SMS. No WhatsApp.
  • Cold outbound only. No inbound nurture, no lead scoring of existing pipeline, no event management.
  • Conference-attendee leads only (for the InspectMind case). Generalizes to "client-provided lead list" but not to "we generate the lead universe from scratch."
  • English language only. No multi-lingual support.
  • US-targeted only. No EU GDPR-compliance work, no APAC localization.
Why this matters

Phillip standup: "We're only doing it on emails. There's no LinkedIn, no Twitter. There's a whole 20 steps that's just this one channel."

Expanding scope = expanding the surface area of bugs, infra costs, and client-iteration time. Wedge A wins by doing email-outbound at top-decile quality before expanding. Every new channel adds another ~10 stages of workflow + 1-2x the infra cost.
