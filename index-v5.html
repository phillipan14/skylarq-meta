v3 was a single-client meta-analysis. v4 was a 4-client portfolio snapshot. v5 keeps the data but reorganizes around the 12 lessons that actually change how we'd run the next 4 engagements. Each lesson cites the clients where we lived it. The historical analysis sits in the archive.

L The 12 lessons

01 1-touch is dead. Multi-touch (3-5) is non-negotiable.
Why it matters

Industry benchmark for cold-outbound reply rate is 0.5-1.5% on a single email vs 2-5% on a 3-5 touch sequence. Most of the lift sits in touches 2-5 (60-70% of all replies come there). One-touch sends look like they're cheap but they leave most of the conversions on the table.

Where we lived it

Smarty IFE Confirmed empirically. 288 sends compressed into 3 days because IFE was 48 hours out. 27.1% open rate (industry-normal), 0 real human replies (the 1 "reply" was JETSET's autoresponder). Variant A still won opens at 30.2% — the framing was good. The sequence shape was wrong.

Skylarq own 0.28% reply rate on 6,072 sends. Most of our own campaigns were 1-2 touch. Same shape, same outcome.

No 1-touch sends going forward unless explicitly scoped by a conference within 48 hours. Default is 3-5 touch over 14-21 days. Smarty IFE got a 2-touch follow-up extension at no cost ("made it right") to validate this — see Lesson 5.
02 Channel mix is ICP-specific, not house style.
Why it matters

Different buyer personas respond to different channels. Selling the same email-first motion to every client is a way to underperform on most of them. Channel selection should be an output of ICP intake, not a Skylarq default.

ICP intake selects the channel mix per niche. Don't default to email-first for every client. Audit the audience first.
03 Conference intelligence is the actual productized motion.
Why it matters

Three of four current clients drove conference-anchored asks within their first weeks. The work pattern is identical each time: research the attendee landscape, identify named buyers, evaluate fit, recommend attend/sponsor/async, build the outreach list. We turned it into a tool.

Where we lived it
  • Denzil IFE attendee research → FGMC fit analysis → FLDC pivot recommendation
  • Emmie Gartner CSO + Snowflake Summit + HITEC three conferences in parallel
  • Jamie / Bayrn (parallel proposal) 4-conference home care comparison (HC100 / HCLS / HCIF / June 15 booth)

Built as Skylarq Discover — Python CLI + Streamlit UI at ~/Developer/skylarq-discover. Four workflows: analyze single conference, compare N conferences, discover via ICP, enrich existing lead list. Parallel Claude Opus 4.7 subagents with server-side web_search. ~$3-8 per run. Each report monetizes standalone at $500-1,500 or bundled into the retainer.

Conference intelligence is the wedge product. The fact that we already productized it (CLI + UI) means we can take on 10× more conference-decision clients without 10× the Phillip-hours.
04 Two infrastructure paths — Path A long-term, Path B fast-launch, hybrid wins most engagements.
Why it matters

Dedicated client domains take 4-6 weeks to warm. That's incompatible with conference-anchored work where the event is in 3 weeks. The choice is real and the right answer is rarely one extreme.

05 "Made it right" mode preserves trust through under-delivery — and often unlocks the bigger deal.
Why it matters

Scope slips happen. The marginal cost of fixing it is small. The marginal cost of losing the client is large. Eat the gap when you under-deliver, communicate why, propose the fix. Trust compounds.

Where we lived it

Smarty IFE Compressed 1-touch send → Denzil flagged "I thought you were running a multi-email campaign" → we owned the gap, offered 2-touch follow-up at no cost. She greenlit immediately. Within 24 hours she introduced Luke (the GTM advisor) and triggered the multi-channel proposal request that's now under review at $1,550-3,600/mo.

InspectMind Initially shipped copy with sales-voice slips in 112 personalization lines. Aakash flagged. We ran a sales-voice scrub on the render pipeline, re-shipped, and credited him 1 round of revision. Engagement kept going.

When scope under-delivers, the fix is free + fast. Communicate clearly, name the gap, propose the make-good before the client has to ask twice. Often it's the move that converts a one-off into a retainer.
06 PA-gatekept inboxes need real-world hooks. Generic openers die.
Why it matters

Senior inboxes (VCs, CFOs, presidents) are read by an executive assistant first. Without a mutual-connection or event reference, the PA filters to spam. The difference between "Hi {first_name}, I'd love to chat about..." and "Saw you at Gartner CSO last week — we have several mutuals including [name]..." is whether your email gets forwarded at all.

Where we lived it

Denzil VC niche Surfaced by Luke (her advisor) explicitly. Every outbound email now ships with a personalization line tied to: a mutual LinkedIn connection / a recent event ("saw you spoke at X") / a portfolio company signal ("noticed [portco] just raised") / a shared affiliation (MIT alum, HBS section X).

Emmie The hyper-personalized 13 IRL openers from her granola/plaud transcripts are the highest-conversion lever we've seen. 13 emails > 100 generic when the content is specific to a real conversation.

No senior-buyer outbound ships without a personalization hook tied to a real-world signal. Mutual connection > event > portco > affiliation > nothing. If the lead has none of these, deprioritize them — don't burn the send.
07 The judgment layer is human and won't automate. Stage 9 (client copy iteration) is the wedge.
Why it matters

Automation makes the volume. Judgment makes the conversions. About 70% of the 20-stage workflow is automatable, but the parts that aren't (ICP definition, copy iteration with the client, reply qualification, strategic call mid-campaign) are exactly where the value lives. AI can't predict what a founder will object to in round 4 vs round 2 — they themselves don't know.

Where we lived it
  • Aakash 5-6 explicit contradictions across 4 formal feedback rounds. ULI20 placement / utm_term split / physical address / sales-voice — each round was meaningful structural change, not word tweaks.
  • Denzil "I don't think these emails are actually still compelling" → full rewrite to her thesis voice. Then "this is less senior leadership, focus more on faster sales / onboarding" → another rewrite. Couldn't have pre-empted either.
  • Emmie 13 IRL emails require human-in-loop synthesis of granola/plaud transcripts into personalized openers. Today's AI extracts content but not context the way Phillip does on a call recap.
  • Vishal Hallucination bugs (NBA = Niles Bolton Associates not basketball; "Lifestyle 37" truncation) caught by client review, not internal QA.
Don't try to automate Stage 9. Build infrastructure around it (the KG, copy-version tracking, render pipeline scrubs) so the human moves fast, but the human stays. This is what makes the agency defensible.
08 ACV-mismatch outbound math is brutal. Performance pricing is the only fix.
Why it matters

At $50 ACV, a $2K-$10K outbound engagement requires 1-2% paid-conversion rate just to break even for the client. Industry median is 0.1-0.5%. The math doesn't pencil at any reasonable execution quality. Flat retainer pricing for low-ACV products burns the relationship — they pay, don't convert, churn, don't come back.

Where we lived it

Aakash · v1 $50 ACV InspectMind, $2K paid for ULI Spring campaign. Needed 40 paid uploads to break even. Even top-decile execution barely pencils. He acknowledged it.

Aakash · v2 (NEW, KG-sourced) The expansion deal that just locked is $1K base + $500/paid customer accepted. 30/60/90 checkpoint clauses where both sides flex — adjust upward on strong conversion, restructure or pause if not. 60-day attribution window with monthly Stripe + PostHog reconciliation. This is the right shape for low-ACV: skin in the game on both sides, restructure-able if the math breaks.

Vishal Also performance-anchored — $500 per qualified meeting. The pilot is structured so we share the conversion risk + upside.

For ACV under ~$5K: refuse, OR shift to performance pricing (base + per-conversion). Aakash v2 is the template. Don't sell $2K-flat-retainers to $50-ACV products — both sides lose.
09 Customer OS / Skylarq Eternal Knowledge Graph is the compounding moat.
Why it matters

Single-engagement agencies start every client from zero context. Our KG ingests every Gmail thread, calendar event, Slack channel, Granola/Plaud transcript across all clients into a queryable similarity-searchable substrate. The longer we run engagements, the more KG context exists. Competitors don't have this. We do.

Where we lived it
  • This v5 doc was synthesized partly from live KG queries against https://dev.skylarq.ai/api/v1. Aakash's locked $1K + $500/paid expansion terms came from KG; v4 had inferred only "$2K paid + expansion pending."
  • Emmie's 13 IRL emails use her granola/plaud transcripts (KG-ingested) to produce hyper-personalized openers — the lever in Lesson 6.
  • Cross-client memory means "what did Aakash say in feedback round 3?" is answered without re-reading 4 days of Slack.

Roadmap (per customer_os/BACKLOG.md): Temporal recurring sync (Gmail/Calendar/Slack on the existing EC2 worker) + Claude Code MCP integration (read-only API keys, SHA-256 hashed, rate-limited per key) exposing query_customers, get_customer, search_knowledge_base, get_customer_timeline tools.

Customer OS is not a product we sell. It's the internal substrate that lets one operator (Phillip + Jack + Mudit) run 4+ parallel engagements without context collapse. Every conversation that flows through it makes the next engagement better. That's the moat.
10 Founder-voice + thesis-driven openers beat generic friendliness.
Why it matters

Cold outbound is fighting for 4 seconds of attention. A specific credential opener ("MIT CS + AI, ex-Microsoft, Harvard Business School") + a sharp thesis ("AI now writes most production code, so custom software is finally cheaper than 8 tools that half-fit") cuts through. Generic "I'd love to grab coffee" framings underperform.

Where we lived it

Smarty IFE A/B/C variant test:

VariantFramingOpen rate
A — thesis-driven"IFE + a thesis on franchise software" + MIT/Microsoft/HBS opener + build-vs-rent30.2%
B — conversational"coffee at IFE, {first_name}?" — softer opener27.1%
C — tangible-value"coffee at IFE" + $100K/yr + 7x faster qualification stat24.0%

Variant A won opens by 6+ points over C. Reply rate is too noisy to call at n=1, but on opens the thesis-driven opener clearly pulled.

For founder-led outbound, lead with the founder's strongest credential + a sharp differentiated thesis. Generic friendliness is a head-fake to "human" voice and underperforms specificity. The sequence still needs warmth in touch 2-3; touch 1 is for cut-through.
11 Reputation isolation: custom domains per client. The Skylarq pool burns out in 2-3 bad cycles.
Why it matters

Sender reputation is a finite, shared resource on any pool. One client running underperforming copy can crater deliverability for the next client. Domain reputation degrades after 2-3 cycles of bad-fit copy. The fix is mandatory: per-client dedicated domains at scale.

Where we lived it

Skylarq pool · April 2 of our own campaigns auto-paused for bounces (May 1-2) at 4.94% bounce rate. Even with internal QA, deliverability isn't solved.

InspectMind v2 The expansion proposal is built on 5 dedicated lookalike domains (inspectmindai.com / getinspectmind.com / etc.), registered under Aakash, warmed for 4-6 weeks. Skylarq pool only used for Wave 2 launch to bridge the warmup gap.

Smarty · trysmartyai.com Domain was previously suspended (Google's risky-domain flag). Will have weak deliverability for 90+ days post-recovery, possibly permanent. Forces Smarty to launch from Skylarq pool until trysmarty's reputation recovers.

At scale, every client gets dedicated warmed domains. The Skylarq pool is for bridging warmup gaps, not steady-state sending. Reputation isolation is non-negotiable.
12 Don't sell channels we don't run. Honest framing wins.
Why it matters

Multi-channel proposals are tempting to over-spec because "more channels = bigger deal." But selling capabilities we don't have in-house creates two failure modes: (a) we scramble to source partners mid-engagement, (b) the channel is wrong for the ICP and bombs. Both burn trust faster than smaller honest proposals.

Where we lived it

Smarty multi-channel proposal v1 Drafted with calls + SMS as Phase 2/3. Honestly framed as "via vendor partner we'd source" since we don't run dialers. Luke (Denzil's GTM advisor) then said unprompted: "none of these guys want a cold call. Doing it would genuinely make them angry." We dropped calls + SMS entirely from the v2 proposal. Saved a credibility hit.

Vishal Different ICP — ortho practice admins answer phones. Phone is in scope for top-tier leads after Phase 1 builds a warm email list, sourced via vendor partner (still external).

SMS deserves its own honest framing: TCPA + state-law minefield, 10DLC carrier registration is 4-8 weeks, phone-number enrichment accuracy is much worse than email, and there's no equivalent of "warming" a phone — sender reputation is binary. We recommend skipping pure cold SMS for almost every B2B niche.

Sell what we can deliver Day 1, label everything else as Phase 2/3 with the honest "via partner" framing. Match channel selection to ICP per Lesson 2. Trust accumulates faster than capability does.

Pricing decision tree · who we take, who we refuse Click to expand · client filter rules

Rule-based client filter. Originally codified May 12 standup; refined after Aakash v2 (performance pricing for low-ACV).

if client.ACV ≥ $100K → take · outcome-based pricing OK

One deal pays for the entire engagement. $5-10K/mo retainer + per-meeting bonus. Example: enterprise SaaS targeting Fortune 500.

if $5K ≤ client.ACV < $100K → take · standard retainer

Cardinal's sweet spot. $2-5K/mo retainer + per-meeting bonus. Need ~5-10% conversion to pencil for both sides. Example: mid-market B2B SaaS, professional services firms. This is also where Smarty Multi-Niche sits at $1,550-3,600/mo.

if client.ACV < $5K → take ONLY with performance pricing (new template post-Aakash v2)

Flat retainers don't work at low ACV — the math breaks. Aakash v2 template: $1K base + $500/paid customer + 30/60/90 checkpoints. Both sides flex on real conversion data. Don't take low-ACV clients on flat retainer; we did it once (Aakash v1) and learned.

special case: pre-revenue YC / VC-backed → maybe · validate by funding, not ACV

VC-backed companies with no ACV yet but real budget. Use $1.5-2K/mo floor + per-meeting → forces them to qualify the spend. Cardinal's $2K floor was useful here.

The Vishal-abuse pattern (still applies)

Clients given unlimited email volume will abuse it. Vishal example: "He seems like the type who'd want to reach out to every VP, then every PM, then everyone on the team. Give him 1K leads, he wants another 1K."

Per-email-inbox pricing creates a natural volume cap. Client controls how many leads they reach by paying for mailbox capacity. Forces ICP discipline.
